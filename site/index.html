The 30-day price rule

Under the EU Omnibus Directive, when a shop shows a reduced price, the “before” price has to be the lowest price charged in the 30 days before the cut.

Latvia’s consumer protection centre (PTAC) has been checking shops on this. I didn’t see a “lowest price in the last 30 days” line on the shop pages I looked at. Items that sit on sale for weeks, or move between offers every 24 hours, are the cases where the rule bites.

The form below shows how the check works. The prices in the box are made up. They are not BeaA’s.

In the example the price dropped to €125 for twelve days in October. A sale starting 13 November that shows €179 as the “before” price overstates the cut: it is really 14%, not 40%. To run this on real prices you need the history first. src/track_prices.py logs the prices once a day from the shop’s public product feed.

How I did it, and what I couldn’t see

  • Prices, tags and filters come from pages 1 to 3 of beaa.eu/collections/all on 13 November 2026. That is 134 products. Page 4 (about ten products), gift cards, beauty products and the styling session are not included.
  • The clothing numbers leave out accessories (belt, bag, cap, socks, eye mask, hair tie).
  • Discount is one minus the sale price over the shown regular price, rounded.
  • No sales, costs or stock. So there is nothing here about margin or about whether the discounts bring in extra sales.
  • Prices change within hours. Everything above is a snapshot.
  • Code, data and tests are in the project folder, so each number can be re-run. The data files are also here: 13 Nov, 28 Oct, filter labels.
